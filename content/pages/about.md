AI Lately covers artificial intelligence through the people who build it. Every hire, departure, promotion, paper, and filing carries a strategic message, and this publication exists to read it. The standing thesis sits in the name: AI is about people, and about what they have been up to lately.

## Masthead

**Ryan Elliott Dennis**, Founder and Editor. He writes the opinion column, sets the editorial standards, and signs every correction. Reach the desk at editor@ailately.com.

**AI Lately Desk** is the byline on reported analysis. Pieces under that byline draw on public filings, company announcements, and reporting from publications of record, and each one lists its sources in AP style.

**An automated newsdesk** produces one daily report from vetted public sources: company newsrooms, SEC filings, public job boards, and arXiv. Those reports carry a visible label, pass the same house style checks as every other piece, and fall under the corrections policy in full.

## Two sections

**Articles** hold the analysis: data-heavy pieces across every category of AI today, from hiring and agent infrastructure to compute, capital, policy, safety, and the crossover between web3 and AI. Each piece opens with a hyperbolic claim powered by one real statistic; the rest of the piece earns that claim with cited evidence.

**The Signal** carries the weekly feed of the biggest hires in AI. One question governs every entry: what capability did this company just buy, and what does it imply about the next twelve months? Each move records person, destination, title, origin, date, and a public source, plus a confidence label of confirmed, reported, or inferred.

## Method

Sources anyone can inspect form the base of the method. Department of Labor disclosure files for LCA and PERM filings show exact titles and wages by employer. SEC Form 8-K, Item 5.02, records executive appointments and departures at public companies. State WARN notices show contraction. Public job boards on Greenhouse, Lever, and Ashby show intent before execution. Company newsrooms and arXiv show what people chose to say and publish. Reporting from Reuters, Bloomberg, The Wall Street Journal, the Financial Times, CNBC, TechCrunch, Axios, and The Information supplies the rest, always with a link.

Facts read as facts, and interpretation reads as interpretation. A departure gets reported as a destination and a date; the reason appears only with two independent sources or a document. Personal data on this site stays within a small allowlist: name, role, employer, public source, and date.

## Independence

AI Lately holds zero equity, tokens, or advisory positions at any company it covers. Sponsors, when they arrive, will see an issue only after publication. Job listings, when they arrive, will carry a sponsored label and buy zero editorial consideration. The full standard lives on the [editorial standards](/editorial-standards) page.

## For machines

Machines get their own doors: the site publishes [llms.txt](/llms.txt) and [llms-full.txt](/llms-full.txt) for language models, a [JSON version of The Signal](/api/signal), an [RSS feed](/feed.xml), and a [sitemap](/sitemap.xml). Quote with attribution and a link to the canonical URL.

## Design

White, by decision. Every chromatic color is a Pantone Color of the Year, from Classic Blue for links to Cloud Dancer for panels, and the colophon in the footer lists each one. Newsreader carries the reading, Inter carries the wayfinding, and JetBrains Mono carries the figures. Press the question mark key anywhere for keyboard shortcuts.
