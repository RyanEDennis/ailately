# Research notes: Brussels Sets the Clock (EU AI Act, Aug 2026)

Note: session-wide WebSearch budget was exhausted before this task began (pre-used by another process). All research below via WebFetch on primary EU Commission sources plus Wikipedia and company blogs.

- EU AI Act entered into force Aug. 1, 2024. Phase-in: prohibitions on unacceptable-risk systems at 6 months (Feb. 2, 2025); GPAI model obligations at 12 months (Aug. 2, 2025); most other operator obligations at 24 months (Aug. 2, 2026); some high-risk requirements originally at 36 months (Aug. 2, 2027). Source: en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Act.
- GPAI Code of Practice published July 10, 2025; voluntary; three chapters (transparency, copyright, safety and security). Signatory Taskforce chaired by the AI Office. Source: Wikipedia + digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai.
- 21 Code of Practice signatories per the AI Office's own list (as of page update July 31, 2026): AI Studio Delta, Aleph Alpha, Almawave, Amazon, Anthropic, Black Forest Labs, Bria AI, Cohere, Domyn, Dweve, Fastweb, Google, IBM, LINAGORA, Microsoft, Mistral AI, Open Hippo, OpenAI, Pleias, ServiceNow, WRITER. xAI signed ONLY the Safety and Security chapter, not the full code. Meta is absent from the list entirely (confirmed by omission on the official AI Office signatory page — no explicit refusal statement found in fetchable sources this session). URL: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai
- "Digital Omnibus" package (which contains the "AI Omnibus") proposed by the European Commission Nov. 19, 2025. The AI Omnibus entered into force July 27, 2026. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/ai-omnibus-enters-force
- Aug. 2, 2026 obligations that DID take effect (per EC press release dated July 31, 2026): transparency requirements only — chatbots/interactive AI must disclose they are AI; AI-edited or AI-generated images/video/audio (deepfakes) must be labeled; AI-generated content must carry machine-readable marks for automated detection. Enforced by the Commission's AI Office plus national authorities (unnamed in source). Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-starts-enforcing-ai-act-rules-and-new-transparency-requirements-2-august
- Crucially, the AI Omnibus DELAYED the harder high-risk obligations: high-risk AI systems under Annex III now apply starting Dec. 2, 2027 (pushed from Aug. 2, 2026); high-risk AI embedded in physical products (Annex I) now applies Aug. 2, 2028. So the "Aug. 2, 2026 high-risk obligations" the original angle assumed did NOT take effect as high-risk conformity duties — only transparency/deepfake rules did. This is the angle correction. Source: same AI Omnibus URL above.
- Other AI Omnibus provisions: extends SME simplified-obligations treatment to "small mid-cap" companies (SMCs); expands regulatory sandboxes, introduces an EU-level regulatory sandbox; simplifies AI literacy requirements; streamlines EU database registration for exempted systems; adds a prohibition on AI systems generating non-consensual sexual or child-exploitation material; permits special data processing to detect/correct algorithmic bias; extends AI Office oversight powers over GPAI models and AI systems embedded in large online platforms. Source: same URL.
- Fines under the Act: Article 5 (prohibited-practice) violations up to €35 million or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher; other operator-obligation violations up to €15 million or 3%; misleading information to authorities up to €7.5 million or 1%. Source: Wikipedia AI Act page.
- AI Office (attached to the European Commission): more than 125 staff across six units — A1 Excellence in AI and Robotics, A2 Regulation and Compliance, A3 AI Safety, A4 AI Innovation and Policy Coordination, A5 AI for Societal Good, A6 AI in Health and Life Science. Recruiting roughly 40 more contractual agents (technology specialists, legal officers, operations specialists, paralegals) with an application deadline of Sept. 8, 2026. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-office
- Member states must designate national competent (market-surveillance) authorities for enforcement; specific country-level authority names were not confirmed in fetchable sources this session.
- Henna Virkkunen is European Commission Executive Vice-President (2024-2029) for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy — the portfolio overseeing digital/AI policy, including an "Apply AI Strategy," a proposed European AI Research Council, and an "EU Cloud and AI Development Act." Source: https://commission.europa.eu/about/organisation/college-commissioners/henna-virkkunen_en
- Natasha Crampton, Microsoft's Chief Responsible AI Officer, published "Innovating in line with the European Union's AI Act" on Microsoft's EU policy blog, Jan. 15, 2025, addressing Microsoft's AI Act compliance approach. Source: https://blogs.microsoft.com/eupolicy/
- Anthropic named Mariano-Florentino (Tino) Cuéllar as its first Chief Global Affairs Officer, announced via Anthropic's own newsroom, Aug. 4, 2026 — a global-affairs remit that includes European and international policy, amid separate U.S.-side tension over a Pentagon "supply chain risk" designation and export controls. Sources: anthropic.com/news and https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/04/anthropic-names-global-affairs-chief-as-trump-tensions-persist.html (provided verified context).
