## hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn
Well-documented precedent (search titles, stable legal history, low 2026-verification risk): Ninth Circuit ruled scraping publicly available LinkedIn profile data does not violate the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (2019 ruling; case remanded after Van Buren v. United States, reaffirmed by the Ninth Circuit in 2022 per Fenwick, "HiQ Labs Scrapes by Again"). Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiQ_Labs_v._LinkedIn ; https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/hiq-labs-scrapes-by-again-the-ninth-circuit-reaffirms-that-data-scraping-does-not-violate-the-cfaa-1 ; https://calawyers.org/privacy-law/ninth-circuit-holds-data-scraping-is-legal-in-hiq-v-linkedin/. Caveat for the piece: the ruling addressed the CFAA specifically — it did not immunize breach-of-contract, breach-of-terms-of-service, or trademark/impersonation claims, and hiQ itself later lost on remand on separate grounds and shut down. Flagging this nuance for "original analysis."
