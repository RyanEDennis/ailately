# Research notes: Google DeepMind's retention playbook

- Google struck a $2.4 billion deal, reported July 11-12, 2025, to license Windsurf's technology and hire its leadership after OpenAI's planned acquisition of the AI-coding startup collapsed; Windsurf CEO Varun Mohan and co-founder Douglas Chen joined Google. https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/11/google-windsurf-ceo-varun-mohan-latest-ai-talent-deal-.html
- Koray Kavukcuoglu named Google's "chief AI architect" June 11, 2025 (CNBC). https://www.cnbc.com/2025/06/11/google-kavukcuoglu-chief-ai-architect.html
- Noam Shazeer returned to Google in 2024 via the Character.AI license-and-hire deal (reportedly $2.7B) to co-lead Gemini, then departed for OpenAI, reported June 18, 2026 (already sourced/used in article 4; reused here with same citations). https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/18/google-gemini-co-lead-noam-shazeer-leaves-for-openai.html
- Aug. 5, 2026 (fetched, TechCrunch): Jeff Dean (Google employee since 1999, its 30th hire, most recently chief scientist), Sanjay Ghemawat (senior fellow/top engineer), Quoc Le (Google Brain founding member) and Oriol Vinyals (senior DeepMind research scientist) left to co-found Discovery Loop, an AI-for-science startup pursuing automated/recursive research loops. Funded by Radical Ventures and Khosla Ventures, with Alphabet itself, Kleiner Perkins, Lightspeed and Doerr Capital participating. https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/05/jeff-dean-and-other-top-ai-researchers-are-leaving-google-to-launch-their-own-startup/
- Same day, Aug. 5, 2026 (fetched, Axios): Demis Hassabis stepped down as Google DeepMind CEO to become DeepMind chairman and take on the title of Alphabet chief scientist, while continuing to lead Isomorphic Labs. No new DeepMind CEO was named; Koray Kavukcuoglu (CTO) was promoted to senior vice president of the unit, reporting directly to Sundar Pichai — a move that reads as designed to preserve continuity as senior researchers depart. Hassabis: he decided it was "the right time... to hand over my day-to-day operational responsibilities" (paraphrased per one-quote-per-response budget). https://www.axios.com/2026/08/05/google-deepmind-demis-hassabis-ai
- Garden leave/noncompete practice: HR Grapevine, April 10, 2025, reported Google gives departing AI staff up to a year's paid leave rather than letting them join rivals immediately; Slashdot covered "aggressive noncompete rules" the same period. https://www.hrgrapevine.com/us/content/article/2025-04-10-google-deepmind-garden-leave-sparks-debate-around-employee-mobility
- Fortune, Aug. 27, 2026 (fetched) — the retention playbook's limits, hard data: hiring-to-departure ratio fell from 12-to-1 (Q2 2023) to 2-to-1 (Q3 2026); DeepMind's EMEA AI market share fell from 49% (2022-23) to 18.6% (2025-26); 13 of 29 authors on the original AlphaFold2 paper have left; of departing researchers, 19.2% specialized in LLMs versus 15.6% of new hires (an expertise-composition gap); destinations of departing researchers: 25% Anthropic, 21% Meta, 14% OpenAI. A former engineer told Fortune researchers had expected a research lab and found themselves building Gemini products instead (paraphrased). https://fortune.com/2026/08/27/google-deepmind-losing-talent-to-rival-ai-labs-startups-new-data-show/

Angle: The playbook (acquihire rivals via Windsurf, promote from inside via Kavukcuoglu, discourage rival-joining via garden leave, reorganize leadership around Hassabis's step-back) is real and coordinated, but Fortune's Aug. 27 data shows it is losing the underlying numbers game — attrition accelerating, not slowing, with real consequences for Google's frontier-research cadence even as Gemini product shipping continues.
