## Microsoft's actual water claim (primary, fetched)
- Microsoft's public pledge: "water positive by 2030." Four pillars per Microsoft's own sustainability page: minimizing water use at datacenters through smarter design; replenishing local watersheds; increasing water access via community partnerships (111 initiatives cited); using AI to improve water efficiency elsewhere (healthcare, agriculture, glacier monitoring) plus shaping supportive public policy.
- Example cited: 1.5 million cubic meters of water saved via AI-powered precision irrigation in Chile's Maipo Basin.
- Notably, the page provides NO baseline consumption figures, no year-over-year progress metrics, and no specific interim replenishment targets — this gap is the crux of a "claim vs. accountability" critique.
- Source: Microsoft Corporate Responsibility, "Water," fetched, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/corporate-responsibility/sustainability/water

## Data center electricity and water consumption (primary-adjacent, via Wikipedia synthesis, fetched)
- IEA: global data center electricity consumption ~415 TWh in 2024 (~1.5% of global electricity), growing ~12%/year over the prior five years; could reach ~945 TWh by 2030 (~3% of global electricity), growing ~15%/year.
- LBNL 2024 report: US data centers were ~4.4% of national electricity consumption in 2023; projected to reach 6.7%–12% by 2028. US has nearly nine times more data centers than any other country. Data centers projected to drive almost half of US electricity demand growth through 2030.
- US energy mix powering data centers (2024): natural gas 40%, renewables 24%, nuclear ~20%, coal ~15%.
- Water: global data center water footprint ~560 billion liters/year, projected to double by 2030; a single 100MW facility can use up to 2 million liters/day. US data centers used ~17 billion gallons in 2024; hyperscale usage projected at 16–33 billion gallons/year by 2028. Virginia data centers consumed ~2.1 billion gallons in 2023, an 86% increase since 2019. Data centers account for ~0.3% of US public water supply overall.
- Grid stress: a 2024 voltage event disconnected ~1,500 MW of data center load simultaneously across ~60 facilities in the PJM region; NERC identified data centers as the single largest driver of projected demand growth. Virginia residential electric bills could rise $14–$37/month by 2030 from data center demand; national electricity prices could rise ~8% by 2030.
- Source: Wikipedia "Data center" (fetched), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_center

## Nuclear power purchase agreements (confirmed, three of four requested)
- Microsoft–Constellation: September 2024 contract to restart the undamaged reactor at Three Mile Island (Constellation's Crane Clean Energy Center). Constellation CEO: Joseph Dominguez, president and CEO. A $1 billion federal loan was announced by the Trump administration in November 2025 supporting the restart. Source: Wikipedia "Constellation Energy" (fetched), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation_Energy
- Google–Kairos Power: broader 2024 agreement for multiple advanced reactors (first by 2030, remainder by 2035); a specific PPA for the Hermes 2 reactor (50 MW) announced August 2025, delivering power via the Tennessee Valley Authority grid — TVA became the first US utility to sign a PPA with an advanced nuclear plant. Power will serve Google data centers in Jackson County, Alabama, and Montgomery County, Tennessee; delivery expected 2030. Source: Wikipedia "Kairos Power" (fetched), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairos_Power
- Amazon–Talen Energy: March 2024, AWS bought Talen's Cumulus data center campus for $650 million, with a PPA drawing power from Talen's Susquehanna nuclear plant (2.5 GW, Talen holds 90% interest and operational control), Susquehanna River, near Berwick, Pennsylvania. Source: Wikipedia "Talen Energy" (fetched), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talen_Energy
